LinkedIn algorithm reality: the first hour determines reach. Posts with 0 engagements after 30 minutes are essentially dead and won't recover. Diagnosis + fix patterns below, derived from the Cheaper Tokens / Cost Engineer flop (2026-06-03, 20 views and 0 engagements in 30 min).

## Common reasons LinkedIn posts flop in the first hour

1. **Mismatch between hook and body.** The opener teases one thing, the body delivers something else. Readers bounce; algorithm picks up low dwell time. Example: opening with a "Goal Engineering" arrow trajectory but the body only discusses cost engineering.
2. **Hedged voice.** "Recent news suggests..." / "This may be ending..." Three hedges in one sentence read as low-confidence. LinkedIn rewards declarative.
3. **Typos.** Multiple typos in a single post signal low effort. Both human readers and the algorithm penalize. The 2026-06-03 post had four ("singificantly," "manging," "kick of," "uand").
4. **Audience condescension.** "For the masses..." or "For those leading the charge..." — segmenting your readers into in-group and out-group loses the out-group's engagement.
5. **Apologetic CTA.** "It's a side project..." undercuts the work being promoted. Readers don't engage with content the author signals as low-stakes.
6. **No kicker, no payoff.** Posts that trail off into "please reach out" don't land. LinkedIn rewards posts that close hard.
7. **Missing comment-bait.** Posts without a closing question rarely get comments. Comments are the algorithm's strongest engagement signal.

## What to do when a post is dead at 30 min

1. **Delete and repost.** Wait 30 minutes after deleting (lets the algorithm clear any residual signal). Repost with the fixes. Risk of duplicate detection is low when significantly rewritten.
2. **Don't edit and hope.** Edits do not reset the algorithm scoring.
3. **Don't wait for organic recovery.** 0 engagements in 30 min = the algorithm has decided. It won't change its mind.

## Strong opening patterns for LinkedIn

- Stat cascade (3-4 stats in parallel)
- Arrow trajectory (concept A → concept B → concept C)
- Contradiction hook ("Token prices have fallen 5-10× a year. AI bills have risen 67×.")
- Direct address ("Marketers, this is the week.")
- Year/era framing ("Three years of X has moved through Y...")

## Body structure that works

- Define what you're introducing if it's new vocabulary (don't drop "Goal Engineering" without explaining what it means)
- Concrete examples / specific stats grounding abstract claims
- A clear pivot from problem to implication
- One earned kicker per post (not three competing punchlines)

## Closing patterns

- Question to drive comments ("What is your team measuring?")
- Forward-pointing claim ("In a year, X will be a normal CFO question.")
- Owned authority ("I put together a working paper on..." — not "I just made this side project")

## Posting tactics for the relaunch

- Post during peak windows (US East 8–10am or 12–2pm typically work best for B2B audiences)
- Within 10 minutes of posting, drop a substantive first comment from your own account (e.g., a specific operational example)
- If you have 2–3 reliable engagers, DM them the link
- No hashtags in the body. 2–3 at the very end if at all (body hashtags suppress reach)

## When to use a mini-article vs a feed post

- **Feed post**: 150–250 words, optimized for the algorithm, lives in the news feed
- **Mini-article**: 250–400 words, can have subheaders, lives in LinkedIn's article publisher but stays scannable
- **Long-form article**: 600–1500 words, full LinkedIn article format, indexed by LinkedIn search, more substantive
- A mini-article is the right pick when you need definitions or multiple sections but the substance fits under ~400 words
