Don't let the framing run ahead of the evidence. Three traps to avoid:

1. **Lagging indicators framed as pattern interrupts.** A measurement that confirms a shift already underway is not the "anchor" of the shift. It is the receipt. Treating it as the cause inverts the logic.

2. **"Most important / most significant" claims without steelmanning alternatives.** Don't call a data point "the most important of the year" unless you can defend it against the other candidates. The default phrasing for a lagging confirmation is "the data is catching up to what we already knew," not "the pattern interrupt."

3. **Methodology before characterization.** Before describing what a data source measures, verify the methodology. Kantar BrandZ is a brand value methodology (financial value plus brand contribution), not a consumer trust score. Calling it a "trust premium" misrepresents what was measured.

**Why:** Vas caught me on the May 18 2026 Marketing Embeddings draft framing Kantar BrandZ as "the most important data point of the year" and "the pattern interrupt that frames the rest of the week," with all other news items (Amazon Alexa, Netflix agents, TikTok MCP, Salesforce Headless 360, Publicis-LiveRamp) framed as responses to it. He called the approach "very dangerous" and "science fiction." The AI-as-consumer-brand thesis was already visible (Super Bowl ads Feb 2026, prior brand rankings, ongoing coverage). Kantar measured the shift, it did not cause it. And BrandZ isn't a trust metric in the first place.

**How to apply:** Before any framing essay or thesis-driven piece, run three checks before drafting.

(1) Is the anchor data measuring something causal or measuring a delayed shift? If delayed, frame as confirmation, not interrupt.
(2) What does the data source actually measure? Read the methodology page, not the press release.
(3) Are the other items in the piece plausibly responses to the anchor, or parallel responses to the same underlying shift? If parallel, do not claim a causal chain.
