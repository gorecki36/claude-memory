Never use the word "just" to describe an event unless it actually happened in the immediate past, i.e., within a few days of writing. Phrases like "the ruling that just made X discoverable" or "the company just announced Y" must be literally true. If the event happened weeks or months ago, drop "just" or replace with a precise date.

**Why:** 2026-05-24. Caught in Marketing Embeddings issue draft. I wrote "the federal ruling that just made consumer AI chats discoverable" in reference to the Heppner ruling, which was decided in February 2026 — three months before writing. Vas: "never use the word 'just' if its not something that just happened." Loose use of "just" creates false urgency and sounds like AI-essay padding.

**How to apply:**
- Before using "just" with a verb, check whether the event is recent enough to warrant it. If the event is more than ~7 days old, cut "just."
- Acceptable: "OpenAI just shipped a new ad format" if announced this week.
- Not acceptable: "the ruling that just made X discoverable" if the ruling was months ago.
- This applies across all ME content, Melina's drafts, and any other writing. Related to [[feedback_quality_standards]] (source-first verification; precise scoping).
