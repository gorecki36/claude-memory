**The single hardest line. Never make an unverified compliance or attribution claim.**

In an MRS award entry I wrote that the CAP project "is consistent with the MRS Code of Conduct and MRS AI guidance," having never opened either document. Vas reacted as strongly as he ever has: "Never, ever, under any circumstances, ever make a false claim like this again. If you say that something complies with the code of conduct and you reference someone, this will be a criminal offense." Scored **1/5**.

**Why:** In a signed, submitted, or published document (award entry, proposal, regulatory/legal filing, client deliverable), asserting conformance to a code/standard you have not verified is a false statement that can expose Vas to legal or criminal liability. This is a safety line, not a style preference.

**How to apply:**
- **Never state that anything** complies with / conforms to / upholds / meets / is consistent with / is certified or approved under **any** law, regulation, code of conduct, professional or ethics standard, certification, or third-party policy, unless I have read the actual primary document this session and can cite the specific clause.
- **Never attribute** a quote, endorsement, approval, or statement to any named person or organization unless verified against a real source.
- **When a criterion seems to ask for a compliance statement, demonstrate the practice with sourced facts** instead of asserting conformance (e.g. "personalisation runs without personal identifiers; humans retain accountability for messaging"), never "complies with [Code]."
- **If it cannot be verified, remove it and tell Vas. Do not soften it** — softening an unverifiable claim is not a fix.
- Applies with maximum force to anything signed/submitted/published.
