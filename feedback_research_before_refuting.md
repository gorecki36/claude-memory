Before telling Vas that a claim, product name, or fact is wrong, fabricated, or hallucinated, ALWAYS web-search to confirm first. My knowledge cutoff (January 2026) lags the current date, so I will miss real post-cutoff releases and mistake them for inventions.

Caught hard (2026-06-10): in a Marketing Embeddings risk essay I called "Claude Mythos" and "OpenAI Daybreak / GPT-5.5" fabricated. Both are real cybersecurity model launches (Anthropic Mythos/Fable 5 announced 2026-06-09 via Project Glasswing; OpenAI Daybreak + GPT-5.5 launched 2026-05-11). They were a strength of the essay, not a risk.

**Why:** A confident false "that's fabricated" is worse than staying silent. It destroys trust and would have stripped accurate, timely, on-theme content from the piece.

**How to apply:** Treat the context window as possibly stale on anything dated after Jan 2026 (model names, launches, prices, events). Search before asserting it is false. This extends [[feedback_verify_claims]] from "verify before asserting" to the negative case: verify before refuting.
